<h3>About the course</h3><p>This course examines Mexico's economic history from the late eighteenth century to the present, with particular attention to the major transformations of the twentieth century. Students build their own view of the Mexican economy: how its institutions emerged and evolved, how its patterns of growth changed, the crises it went through, and how the country has produced, financed, and organized its economic activity, with an eye to the economic dilemmas Mexico faces today.</p><p>The course combines a chronological sequence with thematic discussions, from the economy of New Spain and Independence, through the Porfiriato, the Revolution, and the developmental state, to the 1982 and 1994 crises, structural reform, and Mexico's place in the global economy. Co-taught with Jose Antonio Merchant Cervantes.</p>
<h3>How the course works</h3><p>Each session pairs a short lecture with a group discussion of the assigned readings, so participation is the core of the grade (40%), including a brief “Graph of the Day” presentation. Students also write two short essays (15% each) that pair a historical reconstruction with a public-policy argument, and take a final exam (30%) that rewards arguments built on historical and economic evidence over memorization.</p>
